# Industrial AI Systems

**Outcome:** Turned raw anomalies and video events into copilots that summarize, diagnose, and answer text queries—wired into case management for the plant floor.

One combined case study for Eizen work: forecasting/anomaly detection, LangGraph analytical agents, and video QA—not three competing portfolio cards.

## Problem

Ops teams drown in alerts. Time-series models flag irregularities; video QA catches line delays; neither becomes a decision unless someone stitches root cause, timeline, and ownership. The constraint is **closed-loop ops**: detect → raise a case → explain in language operators trust → query history without rewatching hours of video.

Sensitive side work (therapy-style agents) stays out of the primary story; summarize elsewhere or omit.

## Approach

I owned three connected systems:

1. **Time-series forecasting & anomaly detection** — diagnose irregularities and surface root-cause signals for operational transparency
2. **LangGraph multi-agent copilots** — DeepSeek + RAG over anomaly data for real-time summarization and analytics (LLMs as analytical copilots, not chat toys)
3. **Video QA for chip manufacturing** — **YOLO**-based detection of startkit/material delays; auto-raise cases; store event summaries; enable **text-to-query** over incidents with OpenAI

Secondary: unsupervised “known-unknown” activity detection on surveillance video for real-time anomalous-behavior alerts in dynamic environments.

## What I built

- Engineered forecasting and anomaly models that feed **root-cause analysis**, not only binary alarms
- Developed **LangGraph** multi-agent systems with **DeepSeek** and **RAG** for summarization and BI-style analytics on anomaly streams
- Built video QA for manufacturing with **YOLO**; integrated case management to auto-raise alerts on material/startkit delays
- Stored and processed event summaries so operators can **query video incidents in text**
- Implemented unsupervised known-unknown activity detection for surveillance anomaly alerts

## Engineering notes

1. **Alerts without cases are noise:** Coupling YOLO events to case management forced the model output to carry ownership fields operators could act on.
2. **RAG over incident summaries beats raw video tokens:** Text-to-query worked once events were normalized; feeding full video embeddings was costlier and harder to audit.
3. **What I’d do next:** Offline eval set of golden incidents (alert correctness, summary faithfulness, query answerability); Phoenix/LangSmith traces for the LangGraph path as a portfolio observability still.
